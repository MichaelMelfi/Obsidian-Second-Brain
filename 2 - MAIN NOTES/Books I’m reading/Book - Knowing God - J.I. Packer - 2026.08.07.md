# Part 1 - Know The Lord
### Summary of PART 1

I’ve never read a book like this and, to my delight, it has really taken me by surprise.

This book started by explaining that before learning intricate things, we need to understand the foundation. Packer illustrates this like learning about a volcano rather than individually researching the environment around it, because the volcano is what dictates and changes the environment. In the same way, if we want to understand life, ourselves and Christianity properly, we first need to understand God.

It showed that when someone truly knows God, there should be evidence of that knowledge in their lives. Their fruit, their responses to situations and the way they live reveal whether they actually know God. Daniel is used as an example of someone whose knowledge of God was demonstrated through his actions.

J.I. Packer explains that knowing God is not the same as knowing about God. He uses the example of a horse. Someone can know what a horse looks like, what it eats, how it smells and how beautiful it is, but that is different from actually knowing a particular horse. Someone who knows a horse understands when it is hungry, recognises its behaviour and emotions, knows what it likes and dislikes, recognises its voice and can even anticipate what it might do based on the signs it gives. Packer’s point is that we are called not simply to know facts about God, but to personally know God.

Packer then explains that our ability to know God is ultimately dependent upon God’s grace and revelation. We cannot know God unless God chooses to make Himself known to us. Just as knowing another person depends upon them revealing themselves to us, knowing God depends upon God revealing Himself to us. God has done this because He desires relationship with His people and because He loves us.

Packer then turns to the second commandment and argues that making images of God can diminish our understanding of Him because no physical image can communicate the fullness of God’s character, holiness, greatness and nature. The golden calf demonstrates that even when people intend to use an image to worship God, the image can begin to shape their understanding of who God is. Therefore, we must allow God to reveal Himself on His own terms rather than creating our own picture of Him.

Packer then focuses on Jesus — God the Son incarnate. He expands on John 1 and explains that Jesus is the Word who became flesh. Jesus was fully God and fully man. When Philippians 2 says that Christ “emptied himself,” Packer argues that this does not mean Jesus emptied Himself of His divinity, but that He willingly laid aside His divine glory and humbled Himself by becoming a servant and taking on human form.

Packer then turns to the Holy Spirit, “the Helper.” He explains the order within the Trinity: Father, Son and Holy Spirit, while maintaining that they are one God. There is an order of relationship and submission within the Trinity, with the Son submitting to the Father and the Spirit being sent by the Father and the Son, while all three remain fully God.

Packer also emphasises how easily the Holy Spirit is neglected in Christian thinking and teaching. The disciples depended upon Jesus for guidance, help and direction, and Jesus promised that after He left, the Holy Spirit would come as the Helper. Therefore, Christians should recognise the Holy Spirit as the living presence of God with us and actively depend upon Him for guidance, strength and help.

The way Packer has structured this first section of Knowing God has really taken me on a journey:

1. Why we must look at God
2. How we look at God
3. Who God is
4. What we must do

It feels like the foundation for everything that follows. As Packer explained at the beginning, this is like learning about the volcano before trying to understand the environment around it. If God is the centre of everything, then understanding Him is the foundation for understanding everything else.

### What I’ve learnt personally
What this section has challenged me on:
This has really challenged me to think about what I research and why I am researching it.
It is so easy to become surrounded and suffocated by intricate details and small theological differences and, in doing so, miss the bigger picture of what God is actually saying. I naturally love the small details — the things that make you think, “Oh, that’s cool” — but those things don’t always contribute to transformation.
This is going to change the way I prepare teachings and preach. When I am researching something, I want to continually ask myself:

“Why am I adding this?”
“Is this leading people towards transformation, or am I simply giving them more information?”

I don’t want my teaching to simply make people more knowledgeable about the Bible. I want it to help people know God, understand His Word, respond to Him and be transformed by Him.

## Chapter 4 - The One True God
The Second commandment of making images of God likeness
### Summary (2–3 sentences)
- J.I Packer in this chapter makes the claim that the second commandment points not just to defiling images but any image of Jesus or God in general, whether that is Him on a Cross or Him in all His glory.
- He makes this claim explaining that an image cannot capture all of God nature and Character and Glory all at once. If one makes an image of Jesus on the Cross and suffering then they are missing his divinity and victory and Holiness. If one makes an image of God in all His glory then they are missing His character and transcendence from reality. And thus by always missing out a part of Gods character, nature, divinity then that image is defiling to God because it portrays Him as less than He actually is. 
- He shows this in light of crucifixion paintings but in the example of Exodus and the golden calf, that even though they were doing it to worship God and it might help them in devotion, one will eventually (if always using an image to worship God) start to worship the image of God that the image portrays, not what the word of God says because the Image comes from a worldly mind and doesn't capture all of God.
- And through this, if one always turns to depictions of God then they will ultimately not then listen to God on how He wants to revile himself, but fall back on worldly images and depictions of Himself.
### What I learned about God
- God is so so Holy that nothing can ever capture all of Him.

## Chapter 5 - God Incarnate
### Summary (2–3 sentences)
- J.I Packer explains and expands on the understanding of how Jesus is God. He goes through the multiple arguments of the miracles, resurrection etc but shows that the only argument that should really be debated is the virgin birth. Once one believes in the virgin birth, everything else becomes a no brainer. If God the Son came down and took on form of a man while still fully God, anything He does is possible. Stop focusing on the how something has happened and Focus on the why and the effect of that thing happening.

- He goes through John 1. Expands on what John is really showing that 
1. In the beginning was the word (Eternity) - shows that the word had no beginning.
2. The word was WITH God (Personality )- Shows that the word is distinct from God and has its own personality, not exactly the same because He was ’with’ God 
3. The word WAS God (Deity) - Showing that the word itself is God, he is not a created being, he is divine in nature but distinct in personality
4. Through Him all things were made (Creating) - Shows that the word is Gods creative agent. Showing that he was not created but is the one that everything is created and made through, just as the Father is not created.
5. In Him was life (Animating) - This is showing that in the realm of created things there is no life but only through the word.
6. That life was the light of men (Revealing) - In giving life, he gives light too. Everyone on earth receives intimations of God from the very fact of just being in Gods world.
7. The word became flesh (Incarnation) - The word its self, God Himself was conceived in the Virgin birth in Bethlehem

- He explains how the whole reason for The word coming down was to die. Which he used the anchor verse (2 Corinthians 8:9 ‘for us, He was made poor that through his poverty, we would be made rich) which he end us explaining that this is the calling of Christians, to become poor so we can life others up, we become poor so others can become rich. This points to emptying ourselves, time, money and all we have to help others. 
Side note for myself, This links with Ephesians 5. A husband is called to become poor so that his wife and family become rich, just like Jesus emptied himself out for the church the husband is meant to empty himself out for his wife and family.

- JESUS did not put aside or limit himself when coming down otherwise, he would not be full man and FULLY GOD. He knows what the father tells him, and does what the father wants of Him. He used his divinity sparingly (His Devine powers). For example, when he doesn't know who touched him (lady with the blood).
- When it says He emptied himself, it is not referring to his divinity or power but  referring to his glory. He allowed himself to become a man, be beaten, killed, humiliated so we may become rich.

## Chapter 6 - He shall testify (The Holy Spirit/Godhead)
### Summary (2–3 sentences)
- In this chapter J.I. Packer explains the Godhead and their order. The Son is subject to the will of the Father, the Holy Spirit is subject to the will of the Son and the Father.
- He goes on to show the importance of the Holy Spirit, and how the modern church has neglected the Holy Spirit, which is they neglect the Holy Spirit, they are neglecting God and cannot love Christ.
- There are many points he makes for the importance of the Holy Spirit. He explains that just as Jesus was important for the Disciples as their comforter and guide, we as the church should see the Holy Spirit as the same. The stand in for Jesus when he left. The Holy Spirit does as Christ says just like Jesus did as the Father said when on earth.
- J.I. Packer does make the great point near the end that as Christians we must lean on the Holy spirits power for transform lives, rather than presentation. He anchors this point using (1 Corinthians 2:1-5) as Paul being an example of lowering himself, so Gods powers can be seen even more.
- Our proper response:
1. In our faith = do we truly understand the power and authority of the Bible with reverence.
2. In our life = Do we apply the authority of the Bible? And stand on its convictions and truth no matter what anyone else says?
3. In our witness = Do we truly look to the Holy Spirit to help us witness (Show ourselves as good representative of Christ) to the world?
If we don't do one of these things, we dishonour the Holy Spirit.
### What I learned about God
- Through this chapter it has really opened my eyes and deepened my understanding of the Godhead and how they work. What the purpose of the Holy Spirit is and to really highlight that just as Jesus was extremely important to the disciples, the Holy Spirit must be that to us as our comforter and guide and power.

- Do i really treat Him as my guide and comforter and helper.
- Do i really look at Him as a person?
