# Class Notes: Matthew and the Gospel of Jesus

## Big Picture
- The goal of SBS is to help each person daily fall more in love with Jesus Christ.
- The class framed success as joy, unity, and growing passion for Jesus, not just academic performance.
- Matthew was studied as the final gospel, intentionally ending the year focused on Jesus as King.

## SBS Vision
- Daily fall more in love with Jesus Christ.
- Serve the base and local community.
- Multiply SBS across the South Pacific Islands.
- Train and send Bible teachers into the nations.

## How the Gospels Present Jesus
- Each gospel shows a different angle of Jesus, like different photos of the same person.
- Matthew presents Jesus as the King of the Jews.
- Mark presents Jesus as the Son of Man and focuses on what Jesus did.
- Luke presents Jesus as the Savior for all people, especially emphasizing the poor, women, Gentiles, and the lost.
- John presents Jesus in a more intimate way, focusing on who He is.

## Matthew's Background
- Matthew was also called Levi.
- Levi points to priestly lineage, but Matthew was a tax collector.
- Tax collectors were hated because they worked for Rome and exploited their own people.
- Matthew's response to Jesus was immediate: he left everything and followed.
- His story shows redemption, because the rejected tax collector became the writer of the gospel aimed at the Jewish audience.

## Matthew and Moses
- Matthew intentionally presents Jesus as the new and greater Moses.
- Both Moses and Jesus are connected to childhood threats against male infants.
- Both have links to Egypt and deliverance out of Egypt.
- Moses received the law on the mountain; Jesus teaches and fulfills the law on the mountain.
- Moses led Israel through the sea; Jesus is shown with authority over water and nature.
- Moses brought manna; Jesus multiplies bread and says He is the bread of life.
- The class emphasized that Jesus is better than Moses.

## Old Testament Fulfillment
- Matthew repeatedly ties Jesus to Old Testament prophecy.
- Examples mentioned included:
  - Virgin birth.
  - Messiah coming from Bethlehem.
  - Out of Egypt I called my son.
  - Voice in the wilderness.
  - Light in Galilee.
  - King riding on a donkey.
  - Thirty pieces of silver.
  - Casting lots for Jesus' clothing.

## Structure of Matthew
- Matthew was described as having:
  - An introduction.
  - Five major teaching sections.
  - A conclusion with the passion, resurrection, and Great Commission.
- This structure was compared to the five books of Moses.
- Another way of describing the book was alternating word and deed sections.

## Main Themes in Matthew
- Jesus is the Messiah from David's line.
- Jesus is God with us, Emmanuel.
- Jesus is the new Moses.
- Jesus fulfills Scripture in a very intentional way.
- Matthew uses "kingdom of heaven" because of Jewish reverence for God's name.

## Repentance
- John the Baptist preached: "Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand."
- Jesus preached the same message.
- The class discussed whether repentance should still be central in evangelism.
- Repentance was described as:
  - Turning from sin.
  - Asking forgiveness.
  - Realigning thinking.
  - Renewing the mind to think like Christ.
- The discussion emphasized that repentance is not just moral reform, but a change that turns people toward Jesus.

## Sermon on the Mount Preview
- The class ended by beginning the Sermon on the Mount.
- The Beatitudes were introduced.
- The word blessed was mentioned as meaning happy or joyful in spirit.
- The focus was on how Jesus' kingdom is upside down compared to the world's values.

## Key Takeaway
- Matthew presents Jesus as the promised King who fulfills Scripture, reveals God's character, calls for repentance, and forms His people into joyful Bible teachers.
