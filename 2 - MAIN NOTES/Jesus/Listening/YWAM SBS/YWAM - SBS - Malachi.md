## BRI
### AUTHORSHIP - Who wrote the book?
1. Internal:
The oracle of the word of the LORD to Israel by Malachi. (1:1)
- The name Malachi means “My Messenger”
- It could just be a title, not the name of the prophet
- It is repeated in 3:1
- It is never used in the OT as a name
- Some Jewish traditions say that this was the title for Ezra
- Prophets were often called messengers
- When quoted in the NT, Malachi is never mentioned. When his prophecy is combined with Isaiah’s, it says Isaiah said this, not Malachi
- No genealogy is given
- It could be the name of the prophet
- Most prophets have their names written in the first verse of the book
- The repetition in 3:1 could just be a play on words like in Micah where it says who is a God like you at the end (his name meaning Who is like the LORD?)
- It could be a suitable name for the last prophet to point to the message that will come
- Jar handles have been found from the late monarchy with the name Malachi on it, proving it was an authentic name at the time

### DATING
Part 1: When were these prophecies spoken? 

- No specific person or event is mentioned
- There’s a somewhat chronological ordering of the Minor Prophets with Malachi being the last on
“When you offer blind animals in sacrifice, is that not evil? And when you offer those that are lame or sick, is that not evil? Present that to your governor; will he accept you or show you favor? says the LORD of hosts. (1:8)
- Sacrifices are mentioned. They are abused, so the sacrificial system had to be there for enough time to be abused
- A governor is mentioned. It is the same word that was used for Nehemiah, but it doesn’t mention him, so it probably refers to a pagan ruler. The tone of the passage also implies that.
- The zeal to rebuild and for revival seems to have faded
“Esau I have hated. I have laid waste his hill country and left his heritage to jackals of the desert.” (1:3)
- Nebuchadnezzar attacked Edom in 586BC, but the destruction was not complete. Between 550BC and 400BC, the Arabs did so. The prophecies would need to be given before that.
The sins mentioned in the book (no tithing, intermarriage, no justice, etc) are similar to the sins that Ezra and Nehemiah were dealing with. But Malachi probably did not prophesy while Nehemiah or Ezra were present, because they were pretty quick to deal with sin.
Malachi is quoted in Ben Sirah in 180BC

1. Conclusion:
Malachi most likely prophesied during the absence of Nehemiah when he went to honor his commitment to the king of Persia 12 years after he came to Jerusalem (433B.C.) or after Nehemiah 13 after he would have died. We are not sure how long Nehemiah was in Persia for before coming back and we don’t know when he would have died so we can’t give a more accurate date.

Julian Spriggs - 460 to 435 BC

We don’t have information about the writing of these prophecies, so we can only speculate that it was written shortly after it was spoken.
