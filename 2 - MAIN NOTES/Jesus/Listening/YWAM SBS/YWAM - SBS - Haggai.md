## Big idea
Haggai calls God's people back to covenant relationship, showing that the temple matters because it represents God's presence, God's priority, and God's desire to dwell with his people.

## Historical setting
- Haggai speaks in 520 BC, in the second year of King Darius
- The first group returned from exile around 538/537 BC
- Temple rebuilding began around 536 BC, but then stopped for about 16–17 years
- Haggai is the first prophet to speak to the returned exiles in this period
- Ezra 1–6 gives the main background for the book

## Why the temple matters
- The temple was central to Israel's identity and worship
- It was not just a building project
- It symbolized God living among his people
- The deeper problem in Haggai is not architecture, but misplaced priorities
- The people had started treating their own comfort as more important than God

## Chapter 1: The people's priorities
- The people say it is not yet time to rebuild the house of the Lord
- God challenges them because they live in paneled houses while his house lies in ruins
- Their homes are finished, while God's house has been neglected
- Their choices show self-focus, comfort, and apathy
- They seem to assume there will be no consequence for ignoring God's commands

## Chapter 1: Consequences
- God says they sow much but harvest little
- They eat but are not satisfied
- They drink but are not filled
- They earn wages, but it is like putting them in a bag with holes
- This reflects covenant discipline, not random bad luck
- The drought and lack of fruitfulness are tied to their disobedience

## Chapter 1: The call
- God tells them to go up to the hills, bring wood, and build the house
- The purpose is that God may be glorified and take pleasure in it
- The issue is not merely construction, but obedience and heart posture
- God is calling them back into right relationship with him

## Chapter 1: God's grace
- The people respond in obedience
- God answers with, "I am with you"
- That is one of the most important lines in the book
- Even after years of failure, God does not abandon his people
- His presence is the encouragement they need to keep going

## Chapter 2: Discouragement
- The people may be discouraged because the new temple looks less glorious than Solomon's temple
- The Feast of Booths is important here because it reminded them of God's provision in the wilderness
- God tells them to be strong and keep working because he is still with them
- The message is similar to what God said to Joshua and Solomon
- The point is not comparison, but faithfulness

## Chapter 2: God's presence
- The new temple may seem small, but God's presence is what matters most
- The people should not measure God's faithfulness by outward success
- God is the same God who cared for them in Egypt and the wilderness
- He is still caring for them now
- The reminder is that God's presence matters more than the size or beauty of the temple

## Chapter 2: Shaking the nations
- God says he will shake the heavens and the earth
- This points to divine intervention and future fulfillment
- The silver and gold already belong to God
- The passage is later echoed in Hebrews, which shows its ongoing significance
- The promise looks forward beyond Haggai's immediate moment

## Chapter 2: Holy and unclean
- Haggai uses a lesson about holy and unclean things
- The point is that holiness does not automatically spread, but uncleanness spreads easily
- This teaches that external work alone is not enough
- The people still need cleansing and a right relationship with God
- The issue is inward, not just outward

## Main themes
- God wants relationship, not empty religion
- Comfort and self-focus can replace obedience
- The temple points to God's desire to dwell with his people
- God's presence is more important than appearances or comparison
- Obedience leads to God's "I am with you" presence
- Haggai points forward to the fuller restoration found in Jesus

## Key verses
- Haggai 1:4 — "Is it a time for you yourselves to dwell in your paneled houses, while this house lies in ruins?"
- Haggai 1:5–6 — Consider your ways
- Haggai 1:13 — "I am with you, declares the Lord"
- Haggai 2:4 — "Be strong... and work, for I am with you"
- Haggai 2:9 — The latter glory of this house will be greater

## One-sentence summary
Haggai calls God's people to stop putting themselves first, rebuild the temple, and remember that God's real goal is covenant relationship and his presence among his people.
