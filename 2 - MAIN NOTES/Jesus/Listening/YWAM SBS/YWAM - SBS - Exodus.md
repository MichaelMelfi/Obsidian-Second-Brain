## Wise Counsel & Leadership

### Core Passage
> “Without counsel plans fail, but with many advisers they succeed.” — *Proverbs 15:22*

### Lessons on Wise Counsel
- We must **seek** counsel intentionally; maturity means pursuing advice, not waiting for it.
- A common trap: believing you’re a victim because “no one is pursuing me.”
- The speaker emphasized persistence—reach out repeatedly if needed.
- Quote from his mother: *“The squeaky wheel gets oiled first.”*  
  → Be persistent and humble in seeking wisdom.

### Example: YWAM Montana Illustration
- Older staff wanted to invest in youth but assumed the young were uninterested.  
- Younger staff wanted mentoring but thought the older generation was too busy.  
- Once both sides saw their shared desire, connections formed immediately.
- Challenge: break this same pattern—initiate relationships with older mentors.

## Overview of Exodus

**Why it’s significant**
- The book reveals God as Deliverer and points forward to Jesus.
- It tells the story of God’s power to rescue and dwell among His people.

### Major Themes

#### 1. Liberation from Slavery
- Israel’s rescue from Egypt foreshadows our liberation from sin.
- The pattern: *Bondage → Deliverance → Covenant → Presence.*

#### 2. Sacrifice and Passover
- All were under judgment, but a substitute lamb provided forgiveness.
- The Passover foreshadows Jesus, the Lamb of God (*1 Corinthians 5:7*).

#### 3. God’s Presence
- Thread running from burning bush → plagues → Sinai → tabernacle.
- The tabernacle represented God’s desire to **dwell among** His people.

#### 4. Worship & Service
- In Hebrew, the word for *worship* is the same as *serve* or *slave*.
- Freedom is about *who we serve*, not independence.

#### 5. God’s Missional Purpose
- God told Pharaoh the Exodus would make His name known “in all the earth” (*Ex 9:16*).
- Testimonies of Jethro and Rahab show how Israel’s liberation became a witness.

#### 6. Miracles Do Not Guarantee Faith
- Despite witnessing plagues, manna, and the Red Sea, the Israelites still doubted.
- Faith grows through trust and obedience, not sights and signs.

#### 7. Leadership in Impossible Situations
- Miracles often come **after** circumstances become impossible.
- God leads Moses one step at a time—never revealing the full plan ahead.
- Principle: if you can fix it yourself, you don’t need a miracle.

#### 8. Key Verse on Trust
> “The LORD will fight for you; you need only to be still.” — *Exodus 14:14*

## Moses: Life and Leadership

### Timeline Overview
| Stage | Years | Description |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| Prince of Egypt | 0–40 | Grew up in Pharaoh’s household, highly educated (Acts 7:22) |
| Shepherd in Midian | 40–80 | Exiled after killing an Egyptian; prepared through humility |
| Deliverer of Israel | 80–120 | Led the Exodus, mediated covenant, interceded for Israel |

### Key Stages of His Life
- **Birth and Adoption:** Hidden in the Nile; adopted by Pharaoh’s daughter (Hatshepsut).  
- **Murder and Exile:** Killed an Egyptian oppressor at 40; fled to Midian.  
- **Burning Bush:** Called by God (Exodus 3–4); received divine mission and God’s name “I AM.”  
- **Return to Egypt:** Faced Pharaoh through ten plagues; obeyed without full clarity.  
- **Red Sea Crossing:** Led terrified people between the sea and the army; obeyed, sea parted.  
- **Wilderness Tests:**  
  - Bitter water at Marah → healed.  
  - Manna and quail → daily trust lessons.  
  - Amalek battle → prayer posture brought victory.  
  - Jethro’s visit → delegated leadership to avoid burnout.  
- **Mount Sinai & Covenant:** Received commandments, confronted golden calf idolatry, interceded for mercy.  
- **Tabernacle:** Oversaw its construction; symbol of God’s dwelling among His people.  
- **Intimacy with God:** Spoke to God “face to face” (Exodus 33:11); his face shone with God’s glory.

### Leadership Qualities and Weaknesses
- Faithful in obedience even without full instructions.
- Intercessor for the people, pleading for mercy multiple times.
- Called the most humble man on earth (Numbers 12:3).
- Struggled with anger and frustration (e.g., striking the rock).
- Example: a flawed leader used powerfully by God for His purposes.

### Moses as a Foreshadow of Christ
- Prophet like Moses → *Deuteronomy 18:15; Acts 3:22–23*  
- Mediator between God and Israel → fulfilled by Jesus (1 Timothy 2:5)  
- Deliverer from slavery → fulfilled through salvation in Christ  
- Lawgiver → Jesus fulfilled the Law (Matthew 5:17)

## Historical Setting of Exodus

### Dating the Exodus
- **Early Date (1446 BC):** Based on 1 Kings 6:1 — 480 years before Solomon’s temple (966 BC).  
- **Late Date (1260 BC):** Based on mention of “store cities” (Ex 1:11); popularized by *Prince of Egypt*, but historically weaker.

### Egypt’s Background
- The Nile River made Egypt the “granary of the world.”
- Egyptians worshipped the Nile as a god—hence the first plague.
- Around 90 royal pyramids built (2630–1530 BC), likely involving slave labor.

### Pharaohs in the 18th Dynasty (Early Date View)
| Pharaoh | Possible Role | Notes |
|----------|----------------|------|
| **Thutmose I** | Initiator of Hebrew oppression | Feared growth of Israelite population |
| **Hatshepsut** | Pharaoh’s daughter, adoptive mother of Moses | Ruled 21 years; found Moses in Nile |
| **Thutmose III** | Opposed Moses | Ruthless ruler, potential rival |
| **Amenhotep II** | Likely Pharaoh of the Exodus | Known arrogance; military records stop c.1446 BC |

Pharaohs are unnamed in Exodus because God highlights the faith of ordinary people instead (like the Hebrew midwives and Moses’ mother).

| Section | Chapters | Theme |
|----------|-----------|-------|
| 1–18 | Deliverance | God rescues His people from Egypt |

## Key Takeaways

1. **Wise Counsel is Essential**  
   Seek older voices for wisdom; stop waiting for someone to pursue you.  
   Growth requires humility and initiative.

2. **God Delivers and Dwells**  
   Exodus shows a God who not only rescues but also lives among His people.

3. **Moses is a Model of Steady Obedience**  
   Leadership isn’t perfection—it's hearing God and staying faithful step by step.

4. **Miracles Require Trust, Not Proof**  
   Miraculous signs don’t guarantee faith; true faith thrives in silent trust.

5. **Freedom is Serving God**  
   Worship and servanthood are the same; God frees us so we can serve Him, not self.

6. **Exodus Points to Jesus**  
   The Passover Lamb, the tabernacle, and Moses’ intercession all find fulfillment in Christ.

7. **Don’t Miss the Missional Heart of God**  
   Every act of deliverance in Exodus was designed to make God’s name known among nations.
