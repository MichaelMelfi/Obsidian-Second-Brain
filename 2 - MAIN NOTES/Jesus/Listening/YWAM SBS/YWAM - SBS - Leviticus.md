## Psalm 119 and a Different View of Law
- Psalm 119 is a long meditation on the **law**, treating it as delight, wealth, and life.
- Key phrases about the law:
  - “I will praise you with an upright heart when I learn your righteous rules.”
  - “In the way of your testimonies I delight as much as in all riches.”
  - “Open my eyes, that I may behold wondrous things out of your law.”
  - “My soul is consumed with longing for your rules at all times.”
  - “The law of your mouth is better to me than thousands of gold and silver pieces.”
  - “Oh how I love your law! It is my meditation all the day.”
  - “Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path.”
- All of this is **about the law**, of which Leviticus is an essential heart.
- For Israel:
  - The law was **life**, not a burden.
  - It was the way they could **know God** and how to please him.
  - Without the law, they would not know how to be in relationship with him.
- Challenge for us: we tend to feel “rules and regulations,” but for them it was delight and gift.

## Structure of Leviticus: A Big Chiasm
- The book forms one large **chiasm** (mirror structure) with the **Day of Atonement (ch. 16)** at the center.
- Simplified structure:
  - Offerings – rituals of **sacrifices**.
  - Priests.
  - Clean vs **unclean**.
  - “Love your neighbor” – **moral** laws.
  - Priests again.
  - Feasts/festivals and calendar.
- The Day of Atonement sits in the middle like the **hinge** of the whole book.
- A helpful way to see the halves:
  - First half: largely about **loving God**.
  - Second half: largely about **loving others**.
- Jesus’ summary of the law (“love God… love your neighbor”) comes from **Leviticus** (“love your neighbor as yourself”) and is not a brand-new concept.
- Charting tip:
  - When stuck on interpretation, ask: **How does this show them how to love God?** or **How does this show them how to love neighbor?**

### Alternative Structural Labels (from resources)

- Bible Project version:
  - Ritual sacrifices → priests ordained → ritual purity → **Day of Atonement** → moral purity → priest qualifications → ritual feasts.

## Leviticus in the Pentateuch Story
- Exodus, Leviticus, and Numbers form **one continuous story**.
- Exodus ends:
  - Tabernacle erected on **first day of the first month**.
  - Glory of the Lord fills the tabernacle and **Moses cannot enter**.
  - Problem: God is in the tent; Moses is **outside**.
- Leviticus 1:1:
  - “The Lord called to Moses **from** the tent of meeting” – God inside, Moses outside.
- Numbers 1:1:
  - “The Lord spoke to Moses **in** the tent of meeting” – problem solved.
- All of Leviticus happens in the **one month** between Exodus’ date and Numbers’ date.
- Leviticus answers:
  - How can anyone enter the presence of a holy God without being destroyed?
  - How can God **dwell** among an unholy people?
  - How do we **worship** this holy God?

### Pentateuch as a Whole Chiasm
- The entire Pentateuch also forms a chiasm with **Leviticus at the center**.
- Center of Leviticus = **Day of Atonement** → functional center of the **whole Pentateuch**.
- Link to Exodus 19:6:
  - “You shall be to me a kingdom of priests and a holy nation.”
  - Leviticus unpacks what **kingdom of priests** and **holy nation** look like.

## The Tabernacle: Palace and Eden

### Tabernacle as Palace
- Similarities with an ancient palace:
  - **Gold** everywhere; very costly materials.
  - **King dwells there**; God is presented as Israel’s king.
  - **Throne room**: Holy of Holies with the ark as divine **footstool**.
- The ark:
  - Cherubim on top; God described as enthroned “between the cherubim.”
  - “Mercy seat” has dimensions of a **royal footstool**, not the whole throne itself.
- Materials:
  - Increasing value as you move inward:
    - Bronze outside → silver → **gold** concentrated in the holy place and Holy of Holies.
- Priestly garments:
  - Priests as royal servants in the king’s palace; their garments have a royal flavor.

### Tabernacle as Eden
- Several links between Eden and the tabernacle:
  1. **Materials**:
     - Genesis 2 mentions **gold** and **onyx** in Eden.
     - In the Pentateuch, **onyx** appears only in **Eden** and the **tabernacle**.
  2. **Resource lists**:
     - Exodus 25’s list of tabernacle materials starts and ends with **gold and onyx**, echoing Eden.
  3. **Tree imagery**:
     - The lampstand is designed like a **stylized tree**, with buds and blossoms.
  4. **Direction & cherubim**:
     - Eden:
       - Humanity is driven **east**.
       - Cherubim and flaming sword guard the way back to the **tree of life**.
     - Tabernacle:
       - The entrance faces **east**.
       - The veils have **cherubim woven in**.
       - To “re-enter” God’s presence, you go **westward**, passing cherubim, just as re-entering Eden would require.
- The tabernacle is like a **mini-Eden re-entry**, but still east of Eden:
  - A sign of God’s desire to dwell with his people again.
  - A reminder that the relationship is still not what it was before the fall.

## Why Did God Give the Law?

### Table Discussion Highlights
- Without God’s law, Israel would invent their own laws based on what they **knew**: Egypt.
- Law separates them from:
  - **Egypt** (where they have been).
  - **Canaan** (where they are going).
- Some laws counter **Egyptian** or **Canaanite** religious practices:
  - “Don’t worship like they do.”
- Law also functions in the long-range **plan of redemption**, pointing forward to the cross.
- Law reveals how **holy** God is and how far from him the nations’ practices are.

## Egyptian Religion Background
- Over **1,500 named gods and goddesses**.
- Major gods:
  - Each had their own **temple** with an idol or sacred animal housed there.
  - Temples included a kind of **inner sanctum** with an image on a box; tabernacle parallels, but:
    - Israel’s inner room has **no image**, because the **living God** is there.
- Worship:
  - Priests and worshipers would bring out the idol, **wash, clothe, and feed** it.
  - Anyone could make a home shrine with small figurines.
- Theology:
  - Gods more powerful than humans but **not all-powerful** or all-knowing.
  - Divine favor could **not** be assumed; it had to be **coerced** or manipulated.
- Magic:
  - Central religious activity:
    - Divination, early fortune-telling, dream interpretation.
    - “Seers” who interpreted omens and dreams.
  - Magical **amulets** worn by people of all classes for protection.

---

## Canaanite Religion Background
- Over **230 gods and goddesses**.
- Deified forces of **nature**:
  - Storm, rain, sun, fertility, reproduction.
- Character of the gods:
  - Unpredictable, frequently **angry**, needing appeasement.
  - Could be **manipulated** by sacrifices, magic, and cultic acts.
- Main pantheon (simplified):
  - **El**: high father god (not typically directly worshiped).
  - **Asherah**: El’s consort, mother goddess.
  - Their children include:
    - **Baal**: storm and fertility god.
    - **Astarte/Ashtoreth/Anat**: love/fertility, also war.
  - **Molech**:
    - Known primarily from the Bible.
    - Associated with **child sacrifice**.

### Worship Practices
- **Ritual prostitution**:
  - Especially for Baal and Astarte (fertility gods).
  - Temple prostitution as a way to seek fertility.
- **Sympathetic magic**:
  - Physical act meant to cause spiritual/earthly effect.
  - Example: ritual sex in the temple to secure **fertile crops**.
- Possible link to certain Leviticus laws:
  - Laws against **mixing seeds** and **mixed fabrics** may counter rituals where combining elements symbolized uniting powers for fertility.
- Child sacrifice to **Molech**:
  - Horrific, hence repeated prohibitions: “Do not sacrifice your children.”
- Occult:
  - Witchcraft, omens, divination, and similar practices widespread.

### Law as Counter-Worship
- God repeatedly forbids Israel to worship like the nations.
- Example: **sex and worship separated** sharply in Leviticus:
  - Sexual activity renders one **unclean**, temporarily excluding from tabernacle service.
  - Even priests, though married, must not mix sex and sanctuary service.
  - This directly opposes **Canaanite ritual sex** in temples.

## Types of Law: Case vs Absolute
- Two helpful categories:
  1. **Casuistic (case law)**:
     - Refers to specific conditions or scenarios.
     - Applies to some people, some of the time.
     - Often begins with **“if”** or **“when”**.
     - Example: “If you dig a pit and your neighbor’s ox falls in…”
  2. **Apodictic (absolute law)**:
     - Applies to **all Israelites**, all the time.
     - Usually begins with **“do”** or **“do not”**.
     - Example: the **Ten Commandments**.

### The Law Is Not Exhaustive
- 613 laws is **not** a list of every possible human scenario.
- Law uses examples to set a **standard**, not to cover every variation.
- Example (Leviticus 19):
  - “Do not curse the deaf or put a stumbling block before the blind.”
  - Does this allow harming a lame or mute person? Obviously **no**.
  - The law gives representative examples.
- Another example:
  - “Do not strip your vineyard bare; leave gleanings for the poor and foreigner.”
  - Owners of **olive trees** are **not** exempt; the principle is generosity toward vulnerable people.
- Pharisees later misused the law by treating examples as a **loophole map** instead of principle teaching.

### Heart of the Law
- Even in the OT, the law was meant to be about the **heart**, not mere external performance.
- Deuteronomy will emphasize:
  - The law is **not too hard**.
  - Israel’s main issue is **unwillingness**, not impossibility.

## Holiness: What Makes God Holy?
- Basic meaning of **holy**: distinct, set apart, different from all others.
- For God, key dimensions:
  - **Moral purity**:
    - He is perfectly and completely good.
    - No sin, no mixture, no darkness.
  - **Power**:
    - He is all-powerful; nothing in creation matches his greatness.
  - **Uncreated**:
    - He is not a part of creation; everything else is made, he is not.
- Praise God that he is both **all-powerful** and **all-good**:
  - If he were powerful but not good, this would be terrifying.
  - His holiness is both his absolute **purity** and his overwhelming **greatness**.
