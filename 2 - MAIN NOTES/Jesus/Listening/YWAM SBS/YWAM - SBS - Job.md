## Theodicy
Soul Making Theodicy
- Humanity was put into this world with an undeveloped potential. Suffering is used to grow a person spiritually. Character formation happens when facing pain and suffering

Free Will Theodicy
- God allows man to have free will in order to have freedom. Free will means humanity is free to make their own choices, whether good or evil. Pain and suffering are a result of man's misuse of their freedom and evil actions.

Natural Law Theodicy
- Like gravity, our world has natural laws that are necessary for the world to remain stable and predictable. For example, fire is useful to warm us up, but it can grow into wild bushfires. Natural disasters are a part of these natural laws.

Best of All Possible Worlds Theodicy
- God, in his omnipotence and omniscience, knows all teh combination of all the possible universes that could have been created. He chose the best possible world with the least amount of suffering. Some evil is necessary for the greater good to exist.

Punishment Theodicy
- The pain and suffering that are present in the world are a result of the fall. Humanity and all of creation have been affected by sin, and as a result, the ground was cursed.

A doctor has to put you through pain for you to heal.
This is like God. Sometimes you have to go through pain to heal properly.

### Authorship
The author is unknown. Jewish tradition says that Moses either wrote or
rewrote the book of Job. His sources would have been either oral tradition or
earlier written sources of Job.
A few hints. Since the author uses Yahweh when he refers to God it is assumed
that the author is an Israelite who is using the Covenant name for God. Other
names for God are used in the speeches.
There is a lot of speculation as to the author but this is all that can be confirmed.
Based on the location of Uz and the Teman and Buz, some think that Moses

could have written it when he lived in Midian.
Ezekiel quotes Moses, Job, Daniel
Moses and Daniel were real so it points to Job being real.

### Dating of authorship (When was it written)
Again there is no clear date for the writing of the book. Jewish tradition says that
it was Moses. Some others say that it could have been during Solomon’s reign as
there was an interest in wisdom during Solomon’s reign.
Most agree that Job is very ancient literature.
Job 19:23- Job says that he wished his words were written in a book.
There are no references to Biblical events that would help date the book.
