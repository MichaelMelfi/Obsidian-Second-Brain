# Ecclesiastes: Life, Gift, and Fear of God

## Teaching aim
Ecclesiastes teaches that life under the sun is frustrating, and uncontrollable, but that God still gives real joy in daily life and calls us to fear him, obey him, and receive life as a gift. The book ends by bringing together both honesty about human limits and reverence before God.

## 3. First half: the problem of life

### Vanity or Smoke or useless
Ecclesiastes opens with the repeated word **vanity** or *hevel* - 38X
Vapor, Smoke, and hard to hold/Temporary

Like smoke, you can see it. it appears solid but when you try to grab onto it, it escapes/nothing is there.

“Vanity of vanities... all is vanity” The author is setting the tone for what the Qualet is about to unpack and explain.

The point of the book is not simply that life is worthless, but that it slips away like breath or smoke when you try and hold onto it.

There is so much beauty and goodness is the world but when you try and hold onto it too hard or chase after it, it slips away.

Author describes this as striving after wind (Chasing after wind)
Chase it and it will leave, you cannot grab it (==Sand==)
Like trying to find the end of a ==rainbow==.
Like chasing ==tomorrow== but tomorrow never truly comes because there is always another tomorrow
Chasing the best the most ==ripe fruit==
Like ==chasing a bird== that you can never catch

It doesn't mean its meaningless, but that we will never get the full meaning of it
We are never fully fulfilled with what we Get

### Wealth never satisfies
Ecclesiastes 4:8 and 5:10 
Gaining wealth does not satisfy
Money cannot heal the human heart. 
The appetite grows faster than possessions do, and every new gain just becomes the new normal.

You can say:
- Wealth creates new worries.
- More possession means more protection.
- More comparison means less contentment.
- More money often increases desire rather than satisfaction.

### Work is frustration
Ecclesiastes 2:21-23
You can spend your whole life working hard, becoming wise, building things, and gaining wealth—but when you die, you have to leave it all to someone else who didn’t do any of the work.
Work can become painful, anxious, and empty when it is treated as the place where meaning must be found.

### Death equalizes everyone
Bring in Ecclesiastes 2:16, 3:19-20, and 5:15. 
The wise die like fools, humans and animals share the same breath, and no one takes their possessions with them. Death removes the illusion that we can secure life permanently through success or achievement.

### We cannot control life
Ecclesiastes repeatedly tells us that life does not obey our plans. “What is crooked cannot be made straight” (Eccl. 1:15),
“no man has power to retain the spirit, or power over the day of death” (Eccl. 8:8). 

==This is the first great response of the book: stop pretending life is controllable.==

## 4. Second half: life as gift

### Enjoyment is from God
Ecclesiastes 2:24 
says there is nothing better than to eat and drink and find enjoyment in toil, and that this is from the hand of God. 
Ecclesiastes 5:19 
says wealth and possessions are gifts when God gives the power to enjoy them.

The emphasis is not on pleasure as an end in itself, but on **receiving** life with gratitude. A gift is not grasped or demanded; it is opened and enjoyed. That is why the book can tell us to enjoy daily life without turning enjoyment into idolatry.

A useful application here is to slow down and notice what God has given. Food, work, relationships, beauty, rest, and ordinary daily blessings are not trivial. 

==Ecclesiastes says those things are meaningful precisely because they are gifts, not achievements.==

You can use examples:
- Eat without rushing.
- Work as stewardship, not self-salvation.
- Enjoy people, not just productivity.
- Receive what is in front of you instead of always chasing the next thing.

### The fear of God
The book does not end with enjoyment alone. It ends with reverence: 
“Fear God and keep his commandments” (Eccl. 12:13). 
That fear is not terror but humble awe, worship, and obedience before the God who judges every deed, even hidden ones (Eccl. 12:14).

This is the second response of the book:
- Life is not ours to control.
- Life is a gift to receive.
- God is the Lord to fear.

## 5. Christ connection
Ecclesiastes leaves us feeling the weight of vanity, and that is where the larger biblical story matters. Jesus enters the world’s futility, bears judgment, and gives hope that life is not finally in vain. Ecclesiastes pushes us to look for meaning beyond what we can grasp under the sun.

Eternity in our hearts!

## 6. Closing
Turn to the end of the book

End with the book’s own conclusion in your own words:
- Life is fragile.
- We cannot control it.
- We cannot satisfy ourselves with possessions or achievement.
- But we can receive daily life as a gift from God.
- And we must fear God, because he will judge all things.

A strong final sentence is:
**Ecclesiastes teaches us to stop chasing control, start receiving life as gift, and live in reverent fear before God.**
